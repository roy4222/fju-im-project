Create a throwaway, self-contained HTML prototype for the public FJU Department of Information Management project website. This is a design-direction study, not production code.

Use Traditional Chinese and entirely fictional content. Brand contract: primary navy #003366, deep navy #012243, orange #E56E00, warm orange #FFF4EA, white surfaces, restrained academic-editorial tone, Inter/Noto Sans TC/system fonts. The page must feel related to https://www.im.fju.edu.tw/ without copying it pixel-for-pixel. Do not create a generic gradient startup hero.

Produce one index.html with inline CSS and JavaScript, no CDN/runtime dependency. Put three structurally different variants in the same artifact, selected by ?variant=A, B, or C and a fixed bottom prototype switcher with buttons and Left/Right keyboard shortcuts:

- A: academic editorial brand — strong identity, important news, then curated content.
- B: news and deadlines first — visitors understand current dates and required actions immediately.
- C: project showcase first — past projects and honors lead, while news remains easy to find.

Every variant must expose clear entry points for announcements, recent deadlines, public industry collaborations, past/excellent projects, honors/competitions, project rules, and login. Include at least two working in-memory/read-only interactions such as news-category filtering, project-year filtering, or a mobile menu, with visible state feedback. Responsive acceptance: 1440px desktop and 390px mobile. Use accessible labels, visible focus, semantic headings, and reduced-motion support. Mark the page PROTOTYPE and do not use real people, IDs, contact data, credentials, analytics, billing, MRR, subscriptions, or SaaS charts.

At the top of the source, record the design question: which homepage hierarchy makes the formerly hidden project site genuinely useful? After building, inspect all three variants and fix obvious overflow or broken controls.
